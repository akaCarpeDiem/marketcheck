How each change is measured

Crypto

  • 1h, 24h, 7d, 30d, 1y are rolling windows that end now, as reported by CoinGecko. Crypto trades around the clock, so "24h" always means the last 24 hours.

Stocks, funds, indexes and commodities

  • 1D is the latest price against the previous session's official close. Before the open and at weekends this is the last session's change.
  • 1W and 1M compare the latest price with the close 7 and 30 calendar days earlier, using the nearest trading day on or before that date.
  • YTD compares with the last close of the previous year.
  • 1Y compares with the close one year ago.

Changes are price changes only. They do not include dividends.

Market cap

  • Crypto: price multiplied by circulating supply, from CoinGecko.
  • Stocks: the latest price multiplied by the company's share count. Share counts are taken from Yahoo Finance and rechecked about once a week, so a market cap moves with the price during the day.
  • Funds: assets under management as published by Nasdaq, which is the usual measure of a fund's size. It is rechecked about once a week and moves with the fund's price in between.

Movers, bubbles and the heat map

A list of "biggest movers" is only useful if the moves are real. Thinly traded coins can show enormous percentage changes on almost no money, so they are left out:

  • Crypto movers are picked from the 250 largest coins only, and a coin must have traded at least $2 million in the last 24 hours. Stablecoins, wrapped copies of other coins and tokenized funds whose price barely moves are excluded.
  • Ethereum tokens follow the same rules as crypto, applied to CoinGecko's Ethereum ecosystem list.
  • Solana tokens must have a market cap of at least $2 million and a minimum trading volume for the window shown.
  • Stocks and funds are picked from the fixed lists on the markets page: more than 100 large US-listed companies and 50 major funds.

On the heat map, colour shows direction and strength (green up, red down), and size shows either market value or the size of the move, whichever you choose. Bubble areas are proportional, with a minimum size so the smallest stay readable.

Market hours and dates

The open or closed label follows the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq schedule: regular trading 9:30 AM to 4:00 PM Eastern, Monday to Friday, with pre-market from 4:00 AM and after-hours until 8:00 PM. Holidays and 1:00 PM early closes are worked out from the exchanges' published holiday rules, so the calendar does not run out at the end of a year. An unscheduled closure, such as a national day of mourning, would not be shown until the site is updated.

Federal Reserve decision days are taken from the Federal Reserve's published meeting calendar. Monthly options expiration is the third Friday of each month.

Headlines

Headlines are gathered on our server from the public RSS and Atom feeds of news publishers, including some video channels, and sorted into ten topics: Markets & Economy; Investing; Personal Finance; Crypto & Digital Assets; Real Estate; Technology & Innovation; Policy, Regulation & Politics; Business & Corporate; Commodities & Energy; and Healthcare & Longevity. A story is placed by the feed it came from and by the words in its headline, so now and then one lands in a neighbouring topic.

Each item is also marked as an article, a video or a podcast episode, and you can filter by that. Each one links to the original on the publisher's site. Market Check is not affiliated with these publishers, does not write or rewrite articles, and a headline appearing here is not an endorsement.

Podcasts

The podcast hub is a directory of shows chosen by hand and filed under the same topics. Recent episodes are read from each show's public channel feed about every twenty minutes and open on YouTube. Being listed is not an endorsement of what is said in a show.

When data is late or missing

If a source fails, the page keeps showing the last good figures and says so, with the time they were last updated. If our server cannot reach CoinGecko, your browser asks CoinGecko for the coin list directly. If there is nothing to show, you see a plain message that the data is unavailable instead of an empty box or a made-up number. The time of the latest update is shown at the top of every page.

The daily call

The daily call is a free game: before each US session, guess whether the S&P 500 will finish higher or lower than its previous close. Calls are accepted from one close until the next open at 9:30 AM Eastern. The result is read from the index's closing level; an exact tie counts as neither a win nor a loss. There are no prizes and nothing is staked.

The leaderboard ranks players by how many calls they got right, then by hit rate, then by current streak, for the current month and for all time. Only calls placed while signed in count, and only if our server received them before the session opened; it works out every result itself from the index's closes, so a record cannot be edited in a browser. You appear only if you choose a display name.

Your lists

Your watchlist and portfolio are kept in your browser. If you sign in, they are saved to your account so they follow you between devices. Details are in the privacy policy.

Links to a symbol

Opening a coin, stock or fund puts it in the address bar, for example marketcheck.money/?q=AAPL, so you can bookmark or share it. Addresses like /quote/AAPL lead to the same place.
